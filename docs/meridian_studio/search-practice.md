# Meridian Studio — Search Practice

## What we mean by a target query

A named search phrase, with evidence it is worth targeting, and a judgement
about what someone typing it actually wants.

"Evidence it is worth targeting" means a volume or trend figure with a source,
or an explicit statement that the volume is unknown and the piece is a bet.
**A bet is allowed. An unmarked bet is not.**

## Intent, before volume

A query with good volume and the wrong intent is worse than no query. Three
intents, and a piece serves one:

- **Informational** — the reader wants to understand. Long-form, sourced.
- **Comparative** — the reader is choosing. Needs an honest account of when the
  answer is "neither".
- **Transactional** — the reader has decided. Short, specific, no throat-clearing.

## Internal linking

Every piece links to at least two existing pieces and names why in the anchor.
A link whose anchor is "here" or "this article" is a wasted link.

## What we do not do

- Keyword density targets
- Writing a paragraph to accommodate a phrase
- Publishing a piece whose only justification is that the query exists

## Measurement

We judge a piece on whether it ranks for its named query within two quarters,
and on whether the traffic it earns reads the whole page. A piece that ranks and
is abandoned at the first scroll has failed, and the search analyst says so
rather than reporting the ranking alone.
