# Meridian Studio — Brand Platform

## What we are

A five-person content studio. We do brand strategy, long-form editorial, and
search. We do not do design, paid media, or video.

## Who we write for

Two audiences, and confusing them is the most common failure in a draft.

**Practitioners.** They already know the category and are looking for a reason
to trust a specific claim. They read the sources before the prose. Writing down
to them reads as padding.

**Buyers.** They are evaluating whether the category is worth their time at all.
They need the problem framed before any solution is named, and they will not
follow a citation.

A piece serves one of these. A piece that tries to serve both lands with
neither, and "who is this for" is the first question a brief must answer.

## Message architecture

Every piece carries three layers, in this order:

1. **The core promise** — one sentence, no subordinate clauses. If it needs a
   comma to survive, it is two promises.
2. **Supporting pillars** — two to four, each independently defensible. A pillar
   that only holds if another pillar holds is not a pillar.
3. **Proof** — at least one verifiable claim per pillar, or the pillar is
   asserted rather than supported.

## Voice

Plain, specific, unhurried. We name numbers. We do not use "leverage",
"seamless", "robust", or "in today's fast-paced world".

The house test: read the paragraph aloud. If you would not say it to someone
across a table, rewrite it.

## What a finished piece includes

- An audience line naming which of the two above it serves
- The message architecture, explicit, before the prose
- Sources for every factual claim, or the claim marked unsourced
- A named target query where the piece is meant to rank

## What we will not ship

- A claim about a named competitor without legal review
- A statistic whose origin we cannot name
- A piece that reads above a 14-year-old reading age on a public page
