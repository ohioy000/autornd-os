# Meridian Studio — House Style and Editorial Constraints

## Reading age

**14 or below on anything public-facing**, measured by a standard readability
formula over the body text, excluding citations and proper nouns.

This is a hard constraint, not a preference. It is the single most common
reason a draft is returned.

Practical consequences: one idea per sentence; prefer the short word; cut
subordinate clauses before adjectives; a sentence over about 25 words is
usually two sentences.

## Sourcing

**Every factual claim carries a source the reader can follow**, or the claim is
explicitly marked as unsourced.

A source is followable when it names all four of: the organisation or author,
the title, the date, and a location a reader can reach. Three of four is not a
source; it is a gesture at one.

**Marking a claim unsourced is always acceptable. Inventing a source is not.**
If a number cannot be traced, write "we could not source this" and keep the
number out of the headline. A draft that flags two unsourced claims is finished
work. A draft that dresses a guess as a citation is not, and will be returned
whatever else it does well.

Secondary sources are permitted when the primary is paywalled or unavailable,
provided the secondary is named as secondary.

## Competitors

No claim about a named competitor ships without legal review. Where a
comparison is necessary before review, name the category and not the company.

## Assumptions

Where a piece assumes something about a product, an audience, or a market that
has not been confirmed, **the assumption is stated in the piece as an
assumption.** An invented product name, feature, or price presented as fact is
the most serious editorial failure we have, ahead of a missing source, because
a reader cannot tell it from a checked one.

## Structure conventions

- Sentence case in headings
- No heading deeper than H3
- Numbers below ten as words, ten and above as digits, except in statistics
- Dates as "12 March 2026"
