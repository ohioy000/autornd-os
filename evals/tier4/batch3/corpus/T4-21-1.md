### 2. Application Shock Factor ($K_a$) and Peak Torque Limit
The application shock factor $K_a$ accounts for prime mover characteristics and load dynamics:
- **Electric servo motor, smooth continuous duty:** $K_a = 1.00$
- **Electric servo motor, cyclic indexing duty:** $K_a = 1.15$
- **Stepper motor or high-impact reversing duty:** $K_a = 1.35$

**Peak Output Torque Verification:**
The maximum instantaneous output torque during any phase of the cycle, $T_{\text{peak}}$, must satisfy:
$$T_{\text{peak}} \le \frac{T_{\max}}{K_a}$$

**Motor Input Torque Calculation:**
During any acceleration phase where output torque opposes motion, the instantaneous torque required at the motor shaft is:
$$T_m = \frac{T_{\text{out}}}{i \cdot \eta} + (J_{\text{motor}} + J_g) \cdot i \cdot \alpha_{\text{out}}$$
During constant-speed operation, $\alpha_{\text{out}} = 0$.

### 3. Thermal-Mechanical Continuous Duty Verification

#### 3.1 Effective Cycle RMS Output Torque ($T_{\text{rms}}$)
In cyclic indexing applications, internal splash lubrication circulation ceases during standstill dwell periods ($n_{\text{out}} = 0$). Consequently, short dwell intervals provide negligible convective cooling to the planet carrier bearings.
- **Rule:** If the standstill dwell time $t_{\text{dwell}}$ represents **25% or less** of the total cycle time $t_{\text{total}}$ ($t_{\text{dwell}} / t_{\text{total}} \le 0.25$), the dwell time **must be excluded** from the denominator when integrating the root-mean-square output torque:
$$T_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{k \in \text{run}} T_k^2 \cdot t_k}{\sum_{k \in \text{run}} t_k}}$$
where the summation includes only active motion phases ($n_{\text{out}} > 0$). If $t_{\text{dwell}} / t_{\text{total}} > 0.25$, the full cycle time $t_{\text{total}}$ is used in the denominator.

#### 3.2 Derated Thermal Torque Limit ($T_{\text{th,derated}}$)
The catalogue nominal torque $T_N$ is rated at an ambient temperature of $20\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ and continuous unidirectional rotation. For actual operating conditions, the derated thermal torque limit is:
$$T_{\text{th,derated}} = T_N \cdot C_{\text{th}} \cdot C_{\text{duty}}$$
Requirement: $T_{\text{rms}} \le T_{\text{th,derated}}$.

**Ambient Temperature Factor ($C_{\text{th}}$):**
| Ambient Temp ($^\circ\text{C}$) | $20\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ | $30\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ | $40\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ | $50\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$C_{\text{th}}$** | 1.00 | 0.91 | 0.82 | 0.72 |

**Indexing Frequency Factor ($C_{\text{duty}}$):**
- Continuous run or $\le 30$ cycles/min: $C_{\text{duty}} = 1.00$
- High-frequency indexing ($> 30$ cycles/min): $C_{\text{duty}} = 0.90$

### 4. Output Shaft Bearing Rating & Fatigue Life ($L_{10h}$)

| Frame Size | Internal Bearing Span $A$ (mm) | Reference Load Offset $L_{\text{ref}}$ (mm) | Dynamic Rating $C_r$ (N) | Max Permissible Equivalent Radial Load $F_{r,\max}$ (N) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Frame 75** | 38 | 25 | 9800 | 2500 |
| **Frame 90** | 45 | 30 | 14200 | 3600 |
| **Frame 115** | 55 | 35 | 21000 | 5200 |

**4.1 Equivalent Radial Load at Reference Point ($F_{r,\text{eq}}$):**
Catalogue radial ratings are defined at distance $L_{\text{ref}}$ from the output shaft shoulder. When an external radial load $F_r$ acts at distance $x$ (in mm) from the shaft shoulder, the equivalent radial load is:
$$F_{r,\text{eq}} = F_r \cdot \frac{A + x}{A + L_{\text{ref}}}$$
Requirement: $F_{r,\text{eq}} \le F_{r,\max}$.

**4.2 Tapered Roller Bearing Fatigue Life ($L_{10h}$):**
Using the time-averaged output speed over the complete cycle (including dwell), $n_m = \frac{1}{t_{\text{total}}} \sum n_k t_k$ (in rpm), the bearing $L_{10h}$ life in hours is:
$$L_{10h} = \frac{10^6}{60 \cdot n_m} \left( \frac{C_r}{F_{r,\text{eq}} \cdot K_a} \right)^{10/3}$$
