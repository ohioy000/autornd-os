### 2. High-Altitude Corona Voltage Derating
In unpressurized aerospace compartments above $3000\text{ m}$ ($3.0\text{ km}$) altitude, Paschen-curve air breakdown lowers the partial-discharge (corona) inception threshold across external creepage paths and internal metallized film margins.

**2.1 Altitude Voltage Derating Factor ($C_{\text{alt},V}$):**
For altitudes $H$ between $3000\text{ m}$ and $15000\text{ m}$:
$$C_{\text{alt},V} = 1.000 - 0.015 \cdot \left(\frac{H - 3000}{1000}\right)$$
where $H$ is altitude in meters. (For $H \le 3000\text{ m}$, $C_{\text{alt},V} = 1.000$.)

**2.2 Peak Operating Voltage Requirement:**
To prevent cumulative metallization clearing under repetitive switching transients, the **peak repetitive operating voltage** $V_{\text{pk}} = V_{\text{DC,nom}} + \Delta V_{\text{overshoot}}$ must not exceed the derated working voltage limit:
$$V_{\text{pk}} \le V_{w,\text{derated}} = V_{\text{NDC}} \cdot C_{\text{alt},V}$$

### 3. Multi-Frequency Ripple Current & High-Altitude Thermal Model

#### 3.1 Frequency-Weighted Equivalent Ripple Current ($I_{\text{eff,unit}}$)
Capacitor losses comprise dielectric dipole relaxation at low frequencies ($< 5\text{ kHz}$) and skin-effect eddy losses in the schoopage end-sprayed terminations at high carrier frequencies ($> 20\text{ kHz}$). For a composite ripple current spectrum with harmonic components $I_k$ (in $\text{A}_{\text{rms}}$) at frequencies $f_k$, the total effective bank ripple current referred to $10.0\text{ kHz}$ is:
$$I_{\text{eff,total}} = \sqrt{\sum_k \left(\frac{I_k}{K_f(f_k)}\right)^2}$$
For a bank of $N$ identical capacitors in parallel, the effective ripple current per capacitor is $I_{\text{eff,unit}} = I_{\text{eff,total}} / N$.

**Table 2: Frequency Multiplier $K_f(f) = \sqrt{\text{ESR}_{10\text{kHz}} / \text{ESR}(f)}$**
| Frequency $f$ (kHz) | 0.50 kHz | 1.20 kHz | 5.0 kHz | 10.0 kHz | 20.0 kHz | 40.0 kHz | 80.0 kHz |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Multiplier $K_f(f)$** | 0.6250 | 0.7143 | 0.9100 | 1.0000 | 0.9400 | 0.8333 | 0.7200 |

*(Note: $K_f(1.20\text{ kHz}) = 5/7 \approx 0.7143$ and $K_f(40.0\text{ kHz}) = 5/6 \approx 0.8333$.)*

#### 3.2 High-Altitude Convective Thermal Resistance Multiplier ($M_{\text{alt,th}}$)
At high altitude, reduced air density degrades natural and forced-air heat transfer from the capacitor casing, increasing the effective core-to-ambient thermal resistance:
$$R_{\text{th,eff}} = R_{\text{th,ca}} \cdot M_{\text{alt,th}}$$

**Table 3: Altitude Thermal Resistance Multiplier $M_{\text{alt,th}}$**
| Altitude $H$ (m) | 0 m (Sea Level) | 3000 m | 6000 m | 9000 m | 12000 m | 15000 m |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$M_{\text{alt,th}}$** | 1.000 | 1.120 | 1.260 | 1.425 | 1.620 | 1.850 |

#### 3.3 Core Hot-Spot Temperature ($T_{\text{core}}$)
The dissipated power per capacitor is $P_{\text{loss}} = I_{\text{eff,unit}}^2 \cdot \text{ESR}_{10\text{kHz}}$, and the steady-state core hot-spot temperature is:
$$T_{\text{core}} = T_{\text{amb}} + P_{\text{loss}} \cdot R_{\text{th,ca}} \cdot M_{\text{alt,th}}$$
Requirement: $T_{\text{core}} \le 105.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$.

### 4. Carrier-Frequency Peak-to-Peak Voltage Ripple ($\Delta V_{\text{pp}}$)
For a triangular PWM carrier current of total RMS amplitude $I_{c}$ at carrier frequency $f_c$ flowing into a parallel bank of $N$ capacitors (total capacitance $C_{\text{total}} = N \cdot C_{\text{unit}}$), the peak-to-peak carrier voltage ripple is:
$$\Delta V_{\text{pp}} = \frac{\sqrt{3} \cdot I_c}{4 \cdot f_c \cdot C_{\text{total}}} + 2\sqrt{3} \cdot I_c \cdot \frac{\text{ESR}(f_c)}{N}$$
where $\text{ESR}(f_c) = \text{ESR}_{10\text{kHz}} / [K_f(f_c)]^2$.
